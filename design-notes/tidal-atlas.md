# Tidal Atlas

Space is a slow instrument. Broad, quiet fields carry the first impression; finer marks gather at the edges and reward a closer look. Forms should suggest measured coastlines, submerged contours, and places shaped by repeated passage without resolving into literal diagrams.

Color begins in deep petroleum teal, touched by muted sea-glass light. Surface should feel mineral and atmospheric rather than glossy: a trace of grain, a softened glint, a line half-lost in water. Each hue must be tuned with painstaking care so that detail emerges from shadow without competing with the field.

Scale moves from an almost imperceptible texture to a few expansive arcs. Repeated contour marks create rhythm, then open into negative space. The composition stays asymmetrical and patient, with its brightest passage held away from the point of greatest attention.

The final image should read as an object of sustained looking, not an illustration explaining itself. Every edge, interval, and tonal shift must feel meticulously crafted, the product of deep expertise and countless quiet refinements. Any words, if present at all, are sparse and subordinate to the visual field.
